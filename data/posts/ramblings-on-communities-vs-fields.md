What’s the difference between “field building” and “community building”? I liked this definition that my wife came up with: a “field” is professional (eg “Technical AI Safety”), and building that is the act of eg causing more researchers to exist. Versus, a “community” is a specific group of people who bump into each other repeatedly.

Field building might be more scaleable, impersonal, metrics-driven. Community building feels more cozy, bespoke, vibesy.

With a definition like that, you might think I’m stacking the deck in favor of “community building” — doesn’t this frame presuppose that community is better?

(And, it’s definitely easier for me to reason about community building. Many of my projects lean into a cozy community vibe. Community sure seems to be in short supply in the modern era. People need friends more than they need jobs!)

But there seem to be limits to how far you get with community building. I also care a lot about scaleability, and it seems like communities hit certain limits (famously, Dunbar’s number). One read of where Manifold went wrong is that it never figured out how to be a platform that served multiple communities (like subreddits), vs a single hivemind community that hit its carrying capacity.

Community building also runs into personal limitations — I can only keep track of so many different unique people in my fallible monkey brain. (These days, every time I get to know someone new, I feel like my brain must be evicting somebody else to make room…)

The startups of yore (especially the consumer ones, like AirBnB & Uber) seemed like “communities” for their staff, and “fields” for their users. 

But historically, as startups grow in headcount, they struggle with internal comms and overhead and maintaining product velocity. Have any large tech companies cracked this code?

Or, looking broader than tech: what is the largest community that exists today? Maybe the Catholic church? (Perhaps, Jesus is the best community builder of all time.)

I think AI safety and EA have historically been “communities”, and now find themselves needing or wanting to become “fields”. A bunch of old-school intuitions may not apply — it’s no longer a few hundred people going to each other’s parties.

Perhaps, one way to scale up a field is by creating more and more distinct communities within it. A subfield might be small enough to be its own community.

The internet led to a new kind of community becoming possible: forums, Discord chat, Twitter exchanges. (Also, community for specific kinds of people. Manifold started as an online community; I hear EA & rationalism did too.)

I’m still waiting to see the new modalities of communities that LLMs enable. Matching seems obvious, and eg Waypoint explores this product space — but not transformatively well. (Maybe the Twitter algo already does this well enough?)

These days I also often wonder about building for the AIs. I’m not sure whether building for the “field” or for the “community” of AIs is more appropriate.

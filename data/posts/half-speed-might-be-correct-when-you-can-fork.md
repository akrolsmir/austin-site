Anna Salamon's classic essay "[the correct response to uncertainty is not half-speed](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/FMkQtPvzsriQAow5q/the-correct-response-to-uncertainty-is-not-half-speed)" points out that if you're not sure if your destination is either ahead or behind you, the correct thing to do is not to keep trudging along slowly, but to figure it out where your destination is.

As someone often split between multiple projects, perhaps I do move at half-speed (if not third- or quarter-speed). I thought I might try to defend this mode of operating. But let’s begin with an analogy: deciding for a movement, rather than an individual.

Should AI safety support or oppose Anthropic? Right now, the movement seems to be split, almost bipolar. I just attended [https://banrsi.ai/](https://banrsi.ai/) , where 200 people showed up to protest Anthropic's IPO. At the same time, I'm quite sure that many of the folks there are also trying to cozy up to A\\, to win influence and funding. Rivers of digital ink have been spilled about whether it's ethical to work in any frontier lab. It sure does seem like AIS is moving half-speed here. Should the movement stop and make a single decision?

I argue no. A movement is made up of individual people & orgs, and so gets to “fork”: simultaneously explore branches in parallel. Some of its members can take extremist anti-lab positions, and some can work with (or within) the labs. Maybe this looks like half-speed from the outside, but this might also be the correct way to quickly understand what world we are in.

Certainly, I want to see an AI safety filled with people of dissenting opinions, but still unified by some greater orientation towards moral seriousness on the subject of AI, and love and caring for all persons: past and future, real and hypothetical.

Moreover, a unity and shared framework helps us to conduct positive trade between members. The external protester's actions lends support to the insider's push for more resources for safety, even if the two never meet. A more direct trade might include swapping information at a conference. Each can also see the fruits of the other's actions, and use that to improve their own plans — including, switching sides should the evidence support it. And having these actions stay within the banner of a single movement expands the reach and power of the movement overall.

Okay, grant that this is true for a movement — what about for an individual? Well, I think that personhood and identity is best understood as [coordination across time-slices](https://www.cold-takes.com/what-counts-as-death/) of an individual. When "I" work on multiple things, I'm sharding out my timeslices to pursue different paths, in a portfolio, hits-based way. These forks of myself draw on many of the same resources (money, reputation, network, skillset); should any succeed, they add back to that resource pool.

How to reconcile this with Paul Graham's notion of [keeping one top idea in your mind](https://paulgraham.com/top.html)? Well, even when I was working on a single startup, my day-to-day experience of it was one of being constantly juggling various next steps (implement that feature, onboard that user, hire that employee). The process of skilling up might be: successfully holding on to bigger and bigger ideas in your head. Perhaps now, my "top idea" is less any one specific project or org, and more “the AI safety movement”. Someday, “the world”.

Admittedly, I'm still confused about many subquestions here. When should any member or timeslice choose to explore, vs exploit? How do “you” discover what "your" values are? When is internal reallocation (eg movement infighting) positive-sum vs negative-sum? I look forward to my forks (future timeslices) and my intellectual siblings (you, dear reader) for help with reaching our destination(s).

- Relevant reading:
  - [Paul Graham on Jessica Livingston](http://www.paulgraham.com/jessica.html)

    > Jessica and I were already dating when we started YC. At first we tried to act "professional" about this, meaning we tried to conceal it. In retrospect that seems ridiculous, and we soon dropped the pretense. And the fact that Jessica and I were a couple is a big part of what made YC what it was. YC felt like a family. The founders early on were mostly young. We all had dinner together once a week, cooked for the first couple years by me. Our first building had been a private home.
  - [Paul Graham on kids](http://www.paulgraham.com/kids.html)

    > I hate to say this, because being ambitious has always been a part of my identity, but having kids may make one less ambitious. It hurts to see that sentence written down. I squirm to avoid it. But if there weren't something real there, why would I squirm? The fact is, once you have kids, you're probably going to care more about them than you do about yourself. And attention is a zero-sum game. Only one idea at a time can be the [<u>top idea in your mind</u>](http://www.paulgraham.com/top.html). Once you have kids, it will often be your kids, and that means it will less often be some project you're working on.
    - Ctrl+f + replace “kids” with “partner”
  - [Julia Wise on startup founders and kids](https://juliawise.net/what-startup-founders-should-think-about-before-having-kids/)

    > I assume there’s selection bias in what’s written publicly about this because it feels better to say “Yeah, you can do it!” than the reverse. People don’t want to look like jerks (especially sexist jerks). Parents don’t want their kids to read them saying “This was a mistake.” And more people are going to write a blog post about how they made it all work than about how it all fell apart.
- Note that the last two are about kids — perhaps relationships feel different after a while, and I’m still in the early honeymoon phase
